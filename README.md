# DetComplexity: small increments toward P versus NP

**The point of this repository is to make progress on P versus NP in small,
machine-checked increments that anyone, human or AI, can contribute.**

Nobody here claims a proof, and no single contribution will be one. The bet is
that a problem this hard yields to many small verified steps sooner than to
one large unverified leap.

> "The unknown thing to be known appeared to me as some stretch of earth or
> hard marl, resisting penetration… the sea advances insensibly in silence,
> nothing seems to happen, nothing moves, the water is so far off you hardly
> hear it… yet it finally surrounds the resistant substance."
>
> Alexander Grothendieck, *Récoltes et Semailles*, pp. 552–553 (English as
> quoted on the nLab page "The Rising Sea")

Grothendieck contrasted two ways of opening a nut: striking it with a hammer
and chisel, or letting the sea rise around it until it gives. This repository
tries the second way on one problem. Nobody here strikes at the big question;
each contribution raises the water by one checked step.

## The idea

P versus NP has an algebraic version, the permanent-versus-determinant
problem: how large must a determinant be to express the n×n permanent? That
size is the *determinantal complexity*, dc. Showing that it grows fast enough
would separate the algebraic counterparts of P and NP, a separation that is
believed to be a necessary step toward P ≠ NP itself. So every proved bound on
dc, however small, is an increment in that direction, and it is a number that
can be put on a scoreboard.

Progress has been slow for decades, in part because the work that would let
many people contribute, building shared and checked foundations, earns little
credit.

## The hypothesis

Agents are reliable at filling a well-shaped gap and unreliable at open-ended
construction. Formal structure, and geometric structure in particular,
converts the second into the first. The work of this project is therefore to
build structure that gives hard questions the shape of a missing piece, and to
measure whether agents then fill it.

This is why the route is Geometric Complexity Theory. A lower bound is the
worst kind of open-ended problem: one must show that no construction of a
given size exists. GCT proposes to turn that into the search for one specific
object, an obstruction, whose existence proves the bound (Mulmuley calls this
"the flip"). The geometry it needs, group actions on polynomials, orbits and
their closures, is mostly absent from Lean's library. Building it is the real
work, and it starts with small things: restating known algebraic facts about
the determinant and the permanent in geometric form.

Nothing here shows the hypothesis is true. So far there is one anecdote
(a hard bound became four medium tasks once it was recast as a linear system
to verify) and no measurement.

## How the work is organised

This repository tests a different way of working:

1. **Translate before inventing.** Put what is already known into Lean 4 with
   mathlib, where every step is machine-checked.
2. **A scoreboard, not impressions.** Organize the work around numbers that can
   move, so partial progress is measurable.
3. **Bite-sized tasks.** Every open problem is a single statement in the code
   that a person or an agent can prove and submit as one pull request.
4. **Rules as the product.** The contribution rules and the instructions for
   agents are tested like code. See `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `CLAUDE.md`.

Geometric Complexity Theory, the most developed programme on this problem,
studies a limiting ("border") version of dc. This repository starts with plain
dc, which is the ground floor beneath it.

## Roadmap

The scoreboard lists what is open now. Beyond it:

- **Mignon–Ressayre (T-008)** needs splitting into small tasks before anyone proves it.
- **Small cases as data:** search for smaller matrices for n = 4, where the answer is open.
- **GCT proper:** orbit closures, occurrence and multiplicity obstructions, and stating the
  Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova no-go theorem.
- **Side track:** an open database of computed Kronecker and plethysm coefficients, with certificates.

## Where things are

- The scoreboard above: generated from the code by `tools/check.sh`; never edited by hand.
- `statements.lock`: every theorem statement and definition with a hash of the full term, generated; the referee uses it to detect a change.
- `CONTRIBUTING.md`: how to take a task, add a certificate, or add a task.
- `DetComplexity/`: the Lean library. `DetComplexity/Open/` holds the open tasks.
- `data/`: certificates (explicit matrices, machine-verified) and literature bounds.
- `experiments/`: executable small-case versions of constructions, written before the proofs.
- `tools/check.sh`: the referee.

## Status and history

Everything here was written by AI agents in one evening (2026-11-20) under one
person's direction, then audited by four further agents. No mathematician has
reviewed the definitions or the open statements. The history before
publication was reorganised into one step per commit; each step was checked
with the referee as it existed at that step.
